HORSESHOE BAY RESORT JAZZ WEEKEND ADDS TO BUSY NOVEMBER CALENDAR

November 8, 2026 · Horseshoe Bay Now News Desk

A scheduled Wine, Dine & Jazz Festival on November 6–7 helped anchor a weekend of regional events.

Horseshoe Bay was part of a particularly active Highland Lakes entertainment weekend, with Horseshoe Bay Resort’s Wine, Dine & Jazz Festival scheduled for November 6 and 7. The event appeared alongside concerts, family attractions and live-music programs in the regional weekend calendar.

The festival dates matter: by Sunday, November 8, the scheduled two-day resort program had concluded. This article is a look at the weekend’s published lineup and what it says about local tourism, not a claim that performances are continuing today or that attendance figures have been released.

For Horseshoe Bay, the significance of a resort festival extends beyond music. The city’s visitor economy has long drawn on the lake, golf and scenic Hill Country setting. Food and music events can help create reasons to travel during a time of year when swimming and boating are less central to a weekend trip.

An event anchored at a major resort also highlights the relationship between visitors and the wider community. Guests who arrive for a festival may visit nearby shops, restaurants and attractions. Local residents, meanwhile, gain another entertainment option without making the trip to Austin or San Antonio.

The weekend also included FiestaJAM activities across the Highland Lakes and other scheduled concerts and family outings. Together, the listings show how several communities can contribute to a single regional tourism calendar, even though the events are organized separately and appeal to different audiences.

As the calendar turns toward the holiday season, residents should watch for updated event schedules and confirm reservations directly with venues. The November 6–7 jazz festival is now a completed scheduled event, but it illustrates how Horseshoe Bay’s fall season can be as much about dining and entertainment as the lake itself.
