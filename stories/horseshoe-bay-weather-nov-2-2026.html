Overnight Thunderstorms Give Way to a Cooler, Brighter Week in Horseshoe Bay

Monday, November 2, 2026 • Morning forecast

HORSESHOE BAY, Texas — A warm start to the week will give way to a quick round of rain and thunderstorms overnight Monday into early Tuesday. After the front moves through, Horseshoe Bay, Lake LBJ and surrounding Llano and Burnet counties should enjoy several sunnier, cooler days.

Monday: Warm, Breezy — Then Rain After Midnight

Patchy morning fog should lift early. Most of Monday looks mostly sunny with a high near 84°F. South winds will increase during the day, with gusts potentially reaching around 25 mph. Residents should not mistake the largely pleasant daytime weather for the overnight forecast: the National Weather Service puts the chance of showers and thunderstorms near 90% tonight, mainly after midnight. The overnight low should be near 66°F, with around four-tenths to eight-tenths of an inch of rain possible in some locations, though amounts will vary.

Tuesday Morning: Showers Depart, Cooler Air Arrives

A lingering shower is possible early Tuesday, with the rain chance dropping to about 30% before midmorning. Clouds should give way to sunshine as northwest winds bring in drier air. Look for a high around 74°F and a clear Tuesday night near 52°F.

Wednesday Through Friday: Mostly Sunny and Comfortable

The middle of the week looks favorable for outdoor plans around Lake LBJ. Wednesday should be sunny near 75°F, Thursday sunny near 79°F, and Friday sunny near 74°F. Overnight temperatures generally settle into the low-to-mid 50s.

Weekend Outlook: Slight Rain Chance Saturday

Saturday brings a modest 20% chance of showers with partly sunny skies and a high near 72°F. Sunday currently looks mostly sunny, with a high around 70°F. Check for updated timing before making boating or outdoor plans.

What Residents Should Watch

Monday night into Tuesday morning is the main period to pay attention to changing conditions. Rain and lightning may affect overnight travel; brief heavy downpours can reduce visibility. Secure loose outdoor items ahead of gusty weather, keep weather alerts enabled and never drive across flooded roads. A thunderstorm forecast does not by itself mean a severe thunderstorm warning is in effect.
