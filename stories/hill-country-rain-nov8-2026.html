RAIN RETURNS TO THE HIGHLAND LAKES: A WET SUNDAY AND MONDAY

November 8, 2026 · Horseshoe Bay Now News Desk

The National Weather Service expects repeated showers and embedded storms across the region, with a cooler weekend giving way to a milder week.

The dry stretch is giving way to a very different kind of Sunday in the Highland Lakes. A slow-moving upper-level weather disturbance is drawing moisture into South Central Texas, and the National Weather Service expects periods of rain through Monday. The result is likely to be gray skies, damp roads and temperatures more typical of late fall than the warmer days many residents have recently enjoyed.

In its early Sunday forecast discussion, the Austin–San Antonio weather office described the rain as largely beneficial, while warning that thunderstorms embedded in the broader rain shield could briefly produce heavier rates. That distinction matters: an ordinary, steady rain can soak into the ground, while a stronger cell repeatedly passing over the same area can send runoff toward creeks and low spots.

Burnet Municipal Airport was forecast to reach roughly 62 degrees Sunday and 65 Monday, with a 90 percent chance of precipitation Sunday and continuing rain chances Monday. Those figures are a nearby forecast reference rather than a measurement for every neighborhood in Horseshoe Bay. Conditions around Lake LBJ, Granite Shoals and the surrounding hills can differ from one location to the next.

The rain is also arriving at an inconvenient time for anyone hoping to spend the day outdoors. Golf, boating, construction work and outdoor gatherings may need flexible plans. Lightning, when it occurs, is a reason to get off the lake and away from exposed areas even if a particular storm does not look especially threatening.

The larger story is the transition after the weekend. Forecasters expect the upper-level disturbance to move away, allowing rainfall chances to decrease and afternoon temperatures to recover around the middle of the week. That means the damp spell is not expected to define the entire seven-day forecast.

For residents, the practical approach is to watch the radar and local warnings rather than assuming every hour will be a washout. The same storm system can deliver welcome rain to one part of the Hill Country and a brief burst of troublesome runoff to another. Horseshoe Bay Now will continue to follow the National Weather Service forecast as the event develops.
